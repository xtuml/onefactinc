May 19, 2015: The following is an excerpt from a post to the Executable UML discussion on LinkedIn. The original author recommended reading the full discussion there.

Coming from the heart of xtUML and BridgePoint, One Fact expressed enthusiasm for Papyrus-RT and the future of Executable UML. The xtuml.org community and One Fact planned to participate through source code, models, tests, and reviews of standards and proposals.

At the time, BridgePoint had become fully open source in October 2014. The practical work of making the tool accessible and buildable—including documenting and improving the Eclipse/Java model compiler—was still underway. The post described the team as ready to rejoin the broader Executable UML community.

The proposed strategy was incremental and intended to avoid breaking continuity:

  • Preserve the existing body of xtUML models as valuable intellectual property and as a test base for future Executable UML syntax and semantics.
  • Recognize the continuing value of existing model compilers.
  • Advance the xtUML language and tooling without creating a gap in which models can neither execute nor translate.
  • Move toward open-standard technologies.
  • Work with Papyrus and encourage sensible convergence.
  • Keep the focus on modeling—including modeling the tool itself.

This is a historical statement of the team's thinking in 2015, not a description of the current state of Papyrus-RT or BridgePoint.
