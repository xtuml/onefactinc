--- title: "Modeling Domain Specific Languages" date: 2015-01-12 description: "How executable models can define the vocabulary, behavior, and runtime population of a language tailored to a particular domain." permalink: /modeling-domain-specific-languages/ source_url: "https://xtuml.github.io/onefactnet/modeling-domain-specific-languages/" --- A **domain-specific language (DSL)** is a language shaped around the concepts and activities of a particular field. Rather than requiring users to express their work in general-purpose programming terms, a DSL provides vocabulary that is natural to the domain. The example in the video tutorial below focuses on the language of laboratory biology. Laboratory biologists may work with concepts and actions such as *start*, *sample*, *next sample*, *volume*, *inject*, *extract*, *pause*, and *rinse*. These terms can describe the steps involved in configuring an experiment, such as analyzing blood samples collected for a pharmacokinetic study. xtUML can be used to model the language itself. The language's instructions are represented as classes with operational and state-based behavior. A “program” in that language is then represented by the runtime population of instances of the modeled language. This separates the definition of the language from the individual programs expressed in it: **model the language once, then use it many times**. The original article described applications of this approach in laboratory science, industrial automation, and office-device control. Repeated applications revealed common patterns that could be factored into reusable models and applied across domains.
